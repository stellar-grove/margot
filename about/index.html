Methodology & disclosures

How the pages on this site are made, and what they are not.

Where the numbers come from

  • Prices, financial statements, analyst estimates and company profiles come from Yahoo Finance. They can contain errors, gaps and restatements, and each page says the date its data is as of.
  • The newsletter uses exchange-traded funds as stand-ins for indices and sectors (SPY for the S&P 500, QQQ for the Nasdaq 100, the eleven Select Sector SPDRs for sectors), plus the 10-year Treasury yield and the VIX.

How the writing is made

  • Headlines, tiles and sentences are generated from computed values, with wording chosen by the sign and size of each number. Nothing is drafted ahead of the data.
  • Sections headed Margot's take are personal commentary, written by hand and labelled as such.

How the stock analyses work

  • Peers are chosen from the company's industry (or sector) and narrowed to the five closest in market value. Peer medians are computed from that set, not from published benchmarks.
  • Discounted cash flow. When trailing free cash flow is positive, the model projects five years of free cash flow and adds a terminal value, discounted at a cost of capital built from the stock's beta. When it is not positive, a revenue-driven model starts from consensus revenue and ramps the free-cash-flow margin toward a target in bear, base and bull scenarios. Either way the result depends heavily on the assumptions shown on each page; read it as a range, not a price target.
  • Multiples that are negative or not meaningful (for example a P/E on losses) are shown as a dash.

Mock portfolios

When they launch, mock portfolios will be hypothetical: no real money, no real trades. Their returns will not include taxes, and any trading costs assumed will be stated.
